## Abstract
The purpose of the study was to test a model in which the relationships between dispositional optimism/pessimism with positive/negative self-perceptions of aging were mediated by physical activity and loneliness. Survey data from 2,890 respondents who were at least 65 years of age from the 2020 Health and Retirement Study were analyzed using structural equation modeling. Both self-perceived aging and dispositional optimism were divided into two variables: positive and negative. Physical activity was measured by three items that asked about frequency at three levels of exertion. Loneliness was measured by the shortened three-item version of the UCLA Loneliness Scale. Overall, the model fit indices suggest a range from adequate to good fit. These analyses supported the proposed model, indicating that self-perceptions of aging were influenced both directly and indirectly by dispositional optimism. A significant direct relationship was observed between dispositional optimism and self-perceptions of aging, alongside significant indirect effects mediated by loneliness and physical activity. These findings suggest that the relationship between self-perceptions of aging and dispositional optimism is partially mediated by loneliness and physical activity. This study highlights designing and implementing targeted interventions that integrate both psychological and behavioral components to improve older adults' self-perceptions of aging.
